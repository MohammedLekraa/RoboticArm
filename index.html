Sensorized Glove Controlled Robotic Arm

Design, firmware development, and hardware implementation of a low-latency anthropomorphic teleoperation system utilizing IMU orientation tracking, flex sensor capture, and wireless control.

MICROCONTROLLER ESP32-WROOM-32
COMMUNICATION ESP-NOW / 2.4 GHz
SENSORS MPU6050 + 5x Flex
ACTUATION 6x Servo Motors
Fig 1. Complete system setup showcasing the sensorized glove transmitter and the robotic arm receiver module.

01. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & OVERVIEW

The objective of this project was to construct an intuitive, real-time wireless gesture replication system. The glove captures both finger flexion and wrist orientation, processing the analog and I2C signals locally before transmitting packetized data to the robotic arm receiver.

02. HARDWARE & POWER DESIGN

Power management was a crucial aspect of the electronic design due to the high peak currents drawn by servomotors during rapid movements, which could cause voltage drops and reset the logic circuit.

  • Power Decoupling: Separate power rails for logic (3.3V) and power actuators (5V–6V high current supply).
  • Signal Conditioning: Voltage dividers with precision resistors for the flex sensors to maximize ADC resolution.
  • Noise Reduction: Filtering capacitors placed near the IMU and MCU power pins to eliminate high-frequency ripple.

03. FIRMWARE & SIGNAL PROCESSING

The firmware written in C++ running on FreeRTOS / ESP32 framework handles dual-core operations: core 0 manages sensor polling and filtering, while core 1 handles packet transmission via ESP-NOW.
